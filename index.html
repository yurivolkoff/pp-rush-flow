Description

Built for daily use
This 12-can cooler bag is constructed from rugged 600 denier heather polyester, a durable fabric that stands up to regular use. The fully insulated and waterproof main compartment keeps food and drinks cold, while the large gusseted zippered front pocket and two side mesh pockets offer ample storage for accessories.

Comfortable to carry
An adjustable, non-slip shoulder strap with a comfort pad makes it easy to transport, whether you're heading to work, a picnic, or a trade show. The bag's compact dimensions of 10 x 9 x 10 inches and light 1-pound weight make it convenient to carry and store.

Ready for your brand
Your logo can be applied using embroidery, screen print, or heat transfer, each offering a distinct look. The front and back panels provide ample space for branding, making this cooler a practical choice for corporate gifts and employee kits.

Decoration & Imprint

3 methods

Your logo can be placed on the front or back panel of the cooler bag. Choose from embroidery for a raised, textured stitch, screen print for crisp opaque color, or heat transfer for a smooth, low-profile mark.

EmbroideryFull-color imprint · 1 location included · one-time setup $12.50

Embroidery stitches your design directly into the fabric with colored thread, building the mark from dense rows of stitches. The result is a raised, textured mark with the soft sheen of thread and a slightly dimensional edge. Each shape reads as a clean, saturated block, and the sewn-in design resists fading, peeling, and wear through repeated washing.

Screen printing presses ink through a fine mesh stencil, laying down each color as a separate pass. Each color lays down as its own premixed spot ink, printing solid and consistent across the run. The applied mark is flat and opaque, sitting on top of the material with crisp edges; on heavy coverage, you can feel a slight raised texture where ink builds up. The cured ink is durable and holds up to repeated handling and washing.

Heat transfer prints your design in premixed spot inks onto a film, then bonds that film to the surface under heat and pressure. Each color goes down as its own solid ink, so edges stay crisp and color stays even across the mark. The result reads as a smooth, thin bonded layer — low-profile and flexible enough to move with the fabric, not ink soaked into the cloth.
